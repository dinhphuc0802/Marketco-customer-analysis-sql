# MarketCo Customer Behaviour Analysis

## Executive Summary

The report analyses 21,618 customers and 143,785 contracts to understand how MarketCo's customers behave and where the business can grow.

The key finding is that the number of contracts a customer holds, rather than the value of each contract or how much they communicate with MarketCo, drives how much they spend. Customers over 55 spend as much as 4.6 times what customers aged 18 to 30 spend, with the gap driven by the number of contracts they hold. Tasmania has both the highest spending and the most contracts per customer. International customers communicate the most but spend 36% less than customers in Australian states, because they transact less often. The 1,363 former Gold Members are the least active group, less active even than customers who never tried the membership. Operating system and browser make little difference to customer value, and male customers spend more than female customers largely through more cruise contracts.

The report recommends four actions: a win-back campaign for former Gold Members, incentives for early transactions among younger customers, collecting bounce and conversion rates by platform before investing in any one of them, and a small pilot before expanding international marketing.

About 10% of contracts cannot be linked to a customer record and differ in product mix, so these findings describe the linked 90% rather than all of MarketCo's revenue.

## Findings

### Finding 1: Former Gold Members are less active than customers who never tried the membership

**Customers who once held a Gold Membership but have left are less active than customers who never tried it.**

"Never Gold Member" customers (15,490) are the most active group on both measures: average purchase frequency of 6.35, which is 1.7 times that of the "Former Gold Member" group (3.66), and average spending of $349.03 per customer, 62% higher than the $215.30 of the group that has left. "Active Gold Member" customers (4,765) sit in between, at $340.20.

The pattern holds across communication measures. Former Gold Members show the lowest activity on all four (inbound and outbound, over 24 and 60 months), while Never Gold Members lead on all four. For example, 24-month inbound communication averages 4.63 for Former Gold Members against 8.80 for Never Gold Members. No measure shows Former Gold Members outperforming either of the other two groups.

The cause of Former Gold Members being the weakest group cannot be established with confidence from the available data, but there are two plausible explanations. First, these customers may have stopped seeing value in the membership and reduced their activity before formally cancelling, which would make the cancellation a result rather than a cause. Second, losing membership benefits after cancelling may have led them to buy less, even though they were previously active. The data contains no timeline before and after cancellation, so the direction of causation cannot be determined.

### Finding 2: The spending gap between age groups is large and driven by contract volume

**The spending gap between the highest- and lowest-spending age groups is 4.6 times.**

Spending tends to rise with age, although not in a perfectly straight line. Customers over 55 (9,137 people) spend the most, at an average of $518.57, followed by the 46-55 group (5,240 people, $263.51) and the 31-45 group (4,888 people, $190.90). The two youngest groups spend the least: 18-30 (1,883 people, $112.96) and under 18 (470 people, $119.46), with the 18-30 group slightly lower than the under-18 group.

The gap is linked to two factors that were checked against the data. It does not arise because older customers pay more for the same products: the value of each contract is almost fixed across age groups (for example, Gold Membership is always $67). First, average tenure rises with age, from 5.69 months in the 18-30 group to 19.64 months in the over-55 group, so older customers have had more time to accumulate transactions. Second, and more importantly, the average number of contracts per customer rises sharply with age, from 2.39 in the 18-30 group to 8.43 in the over-55 group, a 3.5-fold increase. The number of transactions, rather than the value of each transaction, is what drives the spending gap.

Affluence level does not explain the gap. The share of customers classed as High or Highest income stays between 29.5% and 36.7% across age groups, with no rising trend.

### Finding 3: No operating system or browser stands out as a priority

**No operating system stands out as a clear priority for development.**

The four operating systems (Android, macOS, Linux and Windows) have similar customer numbers, with a gap of only 3.2% between the highest and the lowest. Browsers follow a similar pattern: Firefox, Opera, Edge and Chrome are evenly distributed, and only Safari stands out, with 30% more users than the other browsers, which may relate to the number of customers using Apple devices. Communication levels do not differ meaningfully across operating systems or browsers, with average inbound communication staying within a narrow range of 7.91 to 8.05 in every group.

Customer value is also similar across platforms. Average contracts per customer range narrowly from 5.92 to 6.06 by operating system and from 5.85 to 6.08 by browser. Average spending differs by 6.7% across operating systems ($326.56 for Windows to $348.52 for macOS) and by 12.0% across browsers ($321.91 for Edge to $360.67 for Firefox).

This evenness may reflect a customer base that is naturally diverse in the devices it uses and does not depend on a single technology group, which is consistent with MarketCo's role as an omnichannel retailer serving many customer segments.

### Finding 4: Communication levels do not reflect spending

**Customer communication levels do not reflect how much customers spend.**

TAS (449 customers) has average communication (inbound 8.19) but the highest spending of all seven states ($407.11), while VIC (5,897 customers) has the lowest communication among the states (7.78) but not the lowest spending ($332.23). Even International (190 customers), the group with the highest communication (9.07), falls in the low-spending end ($218.68), although its small sample means this should be interpreted with caution.

This is logical in nature: communication measures how much contact takes place between MarketCo and a customer, in both directions, while spending measures actual purchasing behaviour, and no direct mechanism forces the two to move together. More contact does not mean customers buy more.

The pattern is consistent with the age analysis. The value of each contract is almost fixed, so the number of accumulated contracts, not the value of each transaction, determines spending. TAS (7.06 contracts per customer) and SA (6.79) lead on both contracts and spending. NSW and VIC, the two largest states, have the lowest average number of contracts among the states (5.89 each), which is why their spending sits in the lower half of the states despite their size.

### Finding 5: Female customers transact more often, but male customers spend more

**Female customers transact more often than male customers, but spend less on average.**

Female customers have a higher average transaction frequency (6.54 contracts per customer) than male customers (6.04), but lower average spending: $349.31 versus $385.30. Male customers spend about 10.3% more despite holding fewer contracts.

The gap of about $36 per customer comes mainly from two commission products. Cruise Commission contributes the most: male customers hold 0.50 cruise contracts per customer against 0.38 for female customers, worth about $23 more per customer. Travel Commission contributes the second largest share: male customers average $68.68 per contract against $55.67 for female customers, 23% more, adding about $13 per customer. Female customers partly offset this through Sport Products, adding about $5 per customer at a fixed $15.00 per contract.

Both commissions are earned by MarketCo from selling cruises and tours, and are not paid by customers directly to MarketCo. The pattern most likely reflects male customers booking more cruises and higher-value tours, rather than any difference in how MarketCo charges by gender.

## Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Run a dedicated win-back campaign for Former Gold Members

MarketCo should run a win-back campaign aimed specifically at the 1,363 customers in the "Former Gold Member" group, separate from campaigns that target entirely new customers. This group averages $215.30 in spending, well below the $349.03 of customers who never tried Gold Membership. Because they have subscribed before, the barrier to returning is likely lower than for customers who are unfamiliar with the product. Specific actions are to survey former members on why they left, and to offer a re-subscription incentive designed for this group instead of a general promotion.

### Recommendation 2: Encourage early transactions among younger customers

MarketCo should introduce an incentive programme that encourages early transactions among customers under 30. The 18-30 group has the shortest tenure (5.69 months) and the fewest contracts per customer (2.39). Because the spending gap across age groups comes mainly from the number of transactions accumulated over time, encouraging younger customers to transact more often in their first year (for example, an incentive on their second and third contracts) could shorten the time needed to reach spending levels similar to older customers, instead of waiting for this to happen over several years.

### Recommendation 3: Do not tilt the development budget toward a single platform

MarketCo should not allocate development budget unevenly across operating systems or browsers on the basis of current customer data. Average contracts per customer vary within a narrow range (5.92 to 6.06 by operating system, 5.85 to 6.08 by browser), while average spending differs by 6.7% between Windows and macOS and by 12.0% between Edge and Firefox. These gaps are small relative to the spread of spending across individual customers, whose standard deviation of roughly $700 to $800 is more than twice the average of about $330. Safari has the most customers (5,305) but average spending ($339.64) below Firefox ($360.67), so a larger customer base does not mean higher value.

Instead, MarketCo should collect bounce rate and conversion rate by platform. These measures reflect the website performance issues that followed the recent update, and the current customer data cannot measure them.

### Recommendation 4: Do not expand international marketing on communication levels alone

MarketCo should not expand its international marketing campaigns solely because international customers communicate frequently. The International group (190 customers, under 1% of the customer base) records the highest 24-month communication levels (inbound 9.07, outbound 54.78), yet its average spending is only $218.68, which is 36% lower than that of customers in Australian states ($342.16). The gap stems from fewer contracts per customer (3.55 versus 6.04). As the state analysis showed, communication levels do not reflect spending, while the number of contracts is the deciding factor.

Three of the most likely explanations do not account for most of the gap. Tenure is only 11% shorter (13.66 versus 15.39 months), the value of each contract is not lower ($61.55 versus $56.64), and the product mix is broadly similar. The spending gap therefore comes from International customers transacting less often.

Before investing, MarketCo should investigate what prevents these highly communicative customers from transacting more often. If a campaign is warranted, it should be piloted on a small scale before any wider rollout. Given the small sample (190 customers), these results should be interpreted with caution.

## Limitations

**Data coverage.** A total of 14,574 contracts (10.1% of all contracts), belonging to 2,672 customer IDs, have no matching record in the customer file, so they cannot be linked to age, gender, state or technology. These contracts are not a random subset. They have a different product mix (Hotel Referral Commission 36.8% against 13.6% for linked contracts, Travel Commission 25.9% against 43.3%) and a higher average value per contract ($69.81 against $56.66). The customer-level findings in this report therefore describe the 89.9% of contracts that can be linked, and should not be read as a complete picture of MarketCo's revenue.

**Causation.** The analysis is observational. The Gold Membership comparison shows that former members are the least active group, but without a timeline of activity before and after cancellation it cannot show whether leaving caused the decline or followed it.

**Sample size.** Several groups are small: International (190 customers), customers with no recorded state (242) and Former Gold Members (1,363). Results for these groups are indicative only. About a quarter of customers (24%) have no recorded gender, and their results were not interpreted.

**Variable quality.** Affluence level appears to describe household or area income rather than individual income, since 35.3% of customers under 18 are classed as High or Highest income. It was therefore not treated as an individual-level measure.

**Measurement.** Total_Paid combines membership fees, commissions earned on sales made on behalf of other organisations, and donations. Spending in this report therefore means amounts received by MarketCo, not what customers pay for goods and services. The dataset also holds no bounce rate, conversion rate or page-level data, so website performance by platform could not be assessed.
